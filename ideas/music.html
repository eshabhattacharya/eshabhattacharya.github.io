Music Onchain

[bio] [ideas]

I recently worked on a music royalty ABS deal where we securitized the future royalty payments of a pool of songs. I realized that labels and publishers take ~60-80% of primary sales and future secondary royalties, with artists only receiving ~10-15%, a difference even more dramatic if the artist signs early in their career. In parallel, I’ve been seeing a lot of buzz in the media about Taylor Swift buying back her masters and drawing attention to the predatory, hyper-long-term contracts that artists agree to early in their careers simply because there is no alternative way to reach the critical mass of fans needed to start making a sustainable income. 

Of course, not everyone can be T-Swift. The music industry has been due for an upgrade for a while - players like Universal and Sony have dominated the space since the early 1900s, enduring through the vinyl/radio era, digital recording/CD boom, early internet/MP3, and even now through the rise of streaming. This got me thinking about how the little guy can have ownership and, naturally, blockchain came to mind.

By directly linking the artist and the listener with terms governed and executed with smart contracts, blockchain could cut out the middleman and the bloated take rates in one swoop. And when you consider how substantial the profit is for middlemen like Universal and Concord, this might finally allow artists, and maybe even listeners, to reap rewards for creating and enjoying good music without being the tiny percentage of artists signed with a top label.

Not to mention, this would also fight against the huge loss of art potential as talented artists in financial distress are forced to quit, leaving only the sliver of people with enough financial cushion to make it in the music industry. Rewarding listeners with things like exclusive merchandise or events for supporting an artist might even encourage people to listen to music outside of the Tik Tok top 50 again!

I’m early in thinking about this, but there are a few companies like Royal and Sound.xyz that are already building web3-native music and revenue opportunities. Right now, I see an opportunity for fully on-chain music with terms/royalties fully governed by smart contracts and artists debuting songs directly to listeners.

Once this hits a critical mass, I think the next step is a fully on-chain music ecosystem including producing/songwriting, managing things like copyright, ads, and streaming, buying and selling concert tickets, making and listening to playlists, and even awards for things like top artist, best album, etc. Middlemen from Universal to Ticketmaster and their aggressive, predatory tactics hinge on ownership to take advantage of the industry for decades. Blockchain and decentralized ownership finally offers a solution that could revolutionize the industry, benefitting both artists and listeners.
